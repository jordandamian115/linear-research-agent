# Tell me a little bit about the sun

## Abstract
This paper asks tell me a little bit about the sun? It is written so a reader can talk through the basics: what the subject is, the core ideas in the order the records support, how each idea leads to the next, and where the account stops. A later sentence is used only when it adds that next point. Claims stay inside the sentences the search returned.

## Introduction
The question guiding this paper is: tell me a little bit about the sun?

The pages move in one direction. They say what the subject is, then the contrast that makes the definition necessary. A reader who reaches the close can restate that sequence.

Read the body as a conversation. Begin with what the subject is. Use the contrast next, when the records set that account beside an ordinary case. Take each later idea only because a retrieved sentence carries the previous one forward. Stop where those sentences stop.

## What the records support
The sun is an ordinary star, one of about 100 billion in our galaxy, the Milky Way. [1] The next record strengthens that opening: The Sun is a yellow dwarf star (or G-type main sequence star, the main stage in a star's life cycle). [2] From what the subject is, the records go next to the contrast with the ordinary case the records name.

Although it may look like a solid sphere (like Earth and some of the other planets in our solar system), the Sun is made of superheated gas called plasma. [2] The same record continues: At such a great distance, you might think this ball of gas has little to do with us—but it is actually one of the most important reasons animals, plants, and even bacteria can live on Earth. [2] The same record continues: As we know, the Sun exerts a lot of gravity on the solar system. [2] From the contrast with the ordinary case the records name, the records go next to how it works.

With the subject named, the records say how it works. Less potent UV rays travel through the atmosphere, and can cause sunburn. [1] Stop after that sentence when you explain this. The records do not supply a further step, and this paper does not invent one.

The same record continues: The sun has extremely important influences on our planet: It drives weather, ocean currents, seasons, and climate, and makes plant life possible through photosynthesis. [1]

The same record continues: The star has six layers: the core, radiative zone, convective zone, photosphere, chromosphere, and corona. [2]

The same record continues: Without the sun's heat and light, life on Earth would not exist. [1]

The same record continues: Most of these layers are composed of hydrogen and helium gas, but traces of other elements are also present, including silicon, neon, and chromium. [2]

The same record continues: Instead, the sun is composed of layers made up almost entirely of hydrogen and helium. [1]

The same record continues: That gravitational force also puts a lot of pressure on the Sun itself. [2]

The same record continues: The vast majority of electromagnetic waves that come from the sun are invisible to us. [1]

The same record continues: That fusion releases energy that travels outward from the Sun's core as heat and light. [2]

The same record continues: The most high-frequency waves emitted by the sun are gamma rays, x-rays, and ultraviolet radiation (UV rays). [1]

The same record continues: The most harmful UV rays are almost completely absorbed by Earth's atmosphere. [1]

The same record continues: The sun also emits infrared radiation—whose waves are a much lower-frequency. [1]

The same record continues: Most heat from the sun arrives as infrared energy. [1]

## Conclusion
A beginner who has followed this order can restate the account without adding to it. The subject is the one named in the opening [1][2]. It is set beside the ordinary case named next to that opening [2]. To talk through the rest, keep that order. Say how it works next [1]. Each of those lines is the next retrieved sentence. The handoff is the order itself. Stop there. These records do not establish more than the sentences above, and they do not replace a fuller treatment. They are enough to hand to another person as a first account, and they mark where that account stops.
